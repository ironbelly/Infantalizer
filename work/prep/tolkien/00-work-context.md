# Work Context — The Lord of the Rings

## Author & era

- J.R.R. Tolkien (1892–1973), English philologist and Oxford academic; served in WWI (Somme). [PROBABLE]
- Written 1937–1949; published 1954–1955 in three volumes (*The Fellowship of the Ring*, *The Two Towers*, *The Return of the King*). [PROBABLE]
- Era framing: WWII-era composition, but Tolkien explicitly rejected a 1:1 wartime allegory reading (Foreword to the Second Edition). [PROBABLE]

## Genre & form

- High fantasy / epic romance; three-volume novel structured as six "books" plus appendices. [PROBABLE]
- Mode: quest narrative threaded with elegy (the passing of the Elder Days);' mythopoeic world-building grounded in invented philology. [PROBABLE]

## Reception & cultural weight

- One of the best-selling novels ever written (>150M copies); formative for the modern high-fantasy genre. [PROBABLE]
- Why faithful adaptation matters: the work's meaning (hope against despair, the corrupting nature of power, redemptive self-sacrifice) is woven into the surface narration, not stated abstractly — adaptation must neither add nor strip it. [PROBABLE]
- Multiple prior adaptations (Bakshi 1978 animated; Jackson 2001–2003 films; Amazon *Rings of Power* TV). These are derivative; none is normative for tier-faithful adaptation. [PROBABLE]

## Known adaptation history

- Bakshi 1978 — rotoscoped; partial (covers ~Fellowship + half of Two Towers). [PROBABLE]
- Peter Jackson 2001–2003 live-action trilogy — the dominant popular-reference adaptation; made significant structural and tonal changes (e.g. expanded Arwen, altered Faramir, heightened battle-scope). [PROBABLE]
- *The Rings of Power* (Amazon, 2022–) — Second-Age-set, not directly comparable to the Third-Age quest narrative. [PROBABLE]
