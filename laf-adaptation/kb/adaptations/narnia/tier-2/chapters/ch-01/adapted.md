# Lucy Looks into a Wardrobe

Once there were four children. Their names were Peter, Susan, Edmund, and Lucy. Because of the war, they left the city on a train. So they went to stay in a big old house in the country.

An old Professor lived there. He had shaggy white hair and a great bushy beard. Mrs. Macready, the housekeeper, kept the house tidy. The Professor was kind, but he did look rather odd. Lucy felt a little shy of him at first.

That first night, the children talked in the girls' room. Edmund was tired and grumpy, so he grumbled at Susan. But everyone was too sleepy to mind for long. Outside in the dark, an owl called and called. "Let us explore tomorrow!" said Peter, thinking of woods and hills and eagles.

But the next morning, rain streamed at the windows. They could not see the woods or the hills at all. "We can explore inside instead," said Peter. So off they went.

It was the sort of house you never come to the end of. First came a long room full of old pictures. Then a suit of armour, shining in the dim light. In a green room, a harp waited quietly in the corner. At last they came to an empty room. In it stood one big wardrobe with a mirror in the door.

"Nothing here!" said Peter. And everyone trooped out again. Everyone but Lucy.

Lucy stayed behind. She wanted to try the wardrobe door. It opened easily, and two little mothballs dropped out. Inside hung rows of long fur coats. Lucy loved the soft feel and the smell of fur. She stepped in among them, deeper and deeper. She left the door open behind her, so it would not click shut.

Lucy walked further in, with her arms stretched out. She waited to feel the hard wooden back. But her fingers touched only more soft coats. "This must be an enormous wardrobe!" she thought.

Then the floor changed. Something soft crunched under her shoes. She bent and touched it. It was cold, and it prickled her fingers. Snow? Something rough scraped her cheek. It felt just like the branches of trees. And far ahead, through the dark, a small light was shining.

A moment later, Lucy stood in the middle of a wood. It was night, and snowflakes drifted all around her. She looked back over her shoulder. Far behind the dark trees, the open wardrobe door still glowed. Everything ahead was strange and hushed and new. Then she looked at the little light again. "I can always go back," she thought. So she walked on toward it, crunch, crunch, over the snow.

The light was a lamp-post, standing right in the middle of the wood. How strange, to find a lamp-post among the trees! Lucy stood beneath it, wondering what to do next.

Then she heard a soft pitter-patter of feet. Someone was coming toward her through the snow. Out of the trees stepped a very strange little person. He was only a bit taller than Lucy herself.

Over his head he held an umbrella, all white with snow. In his other arm he carried brown paper parcels. He looked just as if he had been shopping! But from the waist down he was not like a man at all. His legs were furry like a goat's. And instead of feet he had two neat little hoofs! He had a red woollen scarf and a short pointed beard. Two small horns peeped out of his curly hair.

When he saw Lucy, he gave a great jump of surprise. He dropped every one of his parcels in the snow.

"Goodness gracious me!" exclaimed the Faun.
