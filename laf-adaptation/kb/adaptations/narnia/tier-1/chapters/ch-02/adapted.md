# Tea with the Kind Faun

"Hello," said Lucy to the Faun.

The Faun picked up his parcels. Then he made a little bow.

"Hello, hello!" said the Faun. "Are you a real girl?"

"Yes, I am a girl," said Lucy. "My name is Lucy."

"How nice!" said the Faun. "I have never met a girl. My name is Mr. Tumnus."

"I am glad to meet you, Mr. Tumnus," said Lucy.

"This snowy land is called Narnia," he said. "How did you come here?"

"I came through the cupboard," said Lucy.

"It is cold out here," said Mr. Tumnus. "Would you like some tea? My home is close by."

"Thank you!" said Lucy. "But I cannot stay long."

Mr. Tumnus held his umbrella over them both. They walked arm in arm through the snow. Soon they came to a little cave.

Inside, the cave was warm and cosy. A wood fire glowed. There was a soft rug and two small chairs. "One for me and one for a friend," said Mr. Tumnus.

Over the fire hung a picture. It showed an old Faun with a grey beard. "That is my father," said Mr. Tumnus.

Then he made a lovely tea. There were boiled eggs. There was toast with butter. There was toast with honey. And best of all, there was a cake with sugar on top!

When Lucy was full, Mr. Tumnus told stories. He told about dances in the woods. He told about the deep caves of the friendly Dwarfs. He told about summer, when the woods turned green.

Then he took out a little flute. He began to play. The tune was soft and sweet. It made Lucy feel happy and sleepy all at once.

At last Lucy woke up a little. "Oh, Mr. Tumnus," she said. "I love your song. But now I must go home."

Mr. Tumnus put down his flute. A tear rolled down his nose. Then another. Soon he was crying.

"Mr. Tumnus!" said Lucy. "Please don't cry! What is the matter?"

"Oh, I am a mixed-up Faun," he sobbed. "I have a big worry."

"What worry?" asked Lucy kindly.

"There is a Grumpy Witch here," said Mr. Tumnus. "She makes it snow and snow. She never lets it be Christmas. And she gave me a rule."

"What rule?" asked Lucy.

"She said to bring her any girl I met," he said. "But I could never do that. Not to a friend! You are my friend now."

Lucy gave him a pat. "Then don't be sad," she said. "You will not do it. I know you won't."

"You are right," said Mr. Tumnus. He dried his eyes. "I will take you home instead. Right now."

"Thank you, Mr. Tumnus," said Lucy.

"We must walk softly," he said. "The Grumpy Witch has helpers in the wood."

They went out into the snow. They walked quietly, arm in arm. Mr. Tumnus kept to the shady paths. Soon they came back to the lamp-post.

"Do you know your way now?" asked Mr. Tumnus.

Lucy looked through the trees. Far away she saw a soft light. It was the cupboard door! "Yes," she said. "I can see the way home."

"Off you go, little friend," said Mr. Tumnus. He smiled a happy smile. "And thank you for being so kind."

"May I do one thing?" asked Lucy. She gave him her handkerchief. "You may keep this."

"Thank you!" said the kind Faun. He waved goodbye.

Lucy ran toward the light. Crunch, crunch, crunch! Soon the snow was gone. She felt soft coats. She felt wooden boards. And out she tumbled, into the same empty room.

"I'm here!" Lucy called. "I'm back! I'm all right!"
