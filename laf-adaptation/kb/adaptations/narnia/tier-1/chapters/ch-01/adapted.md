# Lucy Finds the Snowy Land

Once there were four children. Their names were Peter, Susan, Edmund, and Lucy. They went to live far away in a big old house. What a big house! A kind old man lived there. He was the Professor, with wild white hair. Mrs. Macready kept the house tidy. She gave Lucy a biscuit and a smile. Lucy felt safe right away.

Edmund was grumpy and tired that first night. The big house felt new and mixed-up. But next day he felt bright again. The children had a good plan. "Let us explore!" said Peter.

Next day it rained and rained. So the children explored inside. They found a room full of pictures. They found a shiny suit of armour. Then they found a small, empty room. In it stood one big cupboard. It was tall and dark.

"Nothing here!" said Peter. The others all ran out. But Lucy stayed behind.

She pulled the cupboard door. It creaked open. Lucy stepped inside. The coats were soft and furry. They smelled of fur and mothballs. Lucy rubbed her face on them. She walked deeper in. She kept her arms out in front. Where was the back of the cupboard?

Then she felt something new. Crunch, crunch. Something soft was under her feet. It was cold and white. Snow! Something rough brushed her face. It felt like branches. A light shone far ahead.

Lucy stepped out of the cupboard. She stood in a wood at night. Snow fell on her nose. Snow fell on her hair. The trees were dark and tall. But the snow made it bright. She could still see the cupboard door. She could go home.

The light looked warm and soft. Lucy felt excited. She began to walk. Crunch, crunch, crunch. She walked toward the light.

The light was a lamp-post. It stood in the middle of the wood. Lucy had never seen a lamp-post in a wood before. How funny! She stood under its warm glow.

Then she heard a sound. Pit-pat, pit-pat. Someone was coming through the snow. Out of the trees stepped a little person. He was only as tall as Lucy. Curly hair poked out under a red scarf. And look! Little hoofs, just like a goat! Up went his big umbrella. Brown paper parcels swung from his arms. He looked ready for a party.

He saw Lucy. He jumped! He dropped all his parcels. They went bump, bump, bump in the snow.

"Oh!" said the Faun. "Goodness gracious me!"

Lucy laughed. The parcels looked so funny in the snow. The Faun laughed, too. He had a kind face and a warm smile.

"Hello," said Lucy. "I am Lucy."

"Hello, Lucy," said the kind Faun. And he smiled at her.
