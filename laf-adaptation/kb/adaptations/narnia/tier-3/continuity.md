- work: narnia
- tier: 3
- chapter: ch-01

A Tier 3 reader now knows there are four children: Peter, Susan, Edmund, and Lucy.
They have been sent away from London because of the air-raids.
They are staying in the old Professor's large country house.
Mrs. Macready is the housekeeper.
Peter is the eldest child.
Susan is practical and looks after Edmund.
Edmund is prickly, sarcastic, and bad-tempered in this chapter.
His bad temper is shown as his own behavior.
The story also names tiredness and rain as causes for his mood.
This honestly seeds Edmund's later betrayal arc without pre-judging him.
Lucy is curious, brave, and careful.
She enters a wardrobe after the others leave the room.
The wardrobe opens into a snowy wood at night.
Lucy keeps the wardrobe door in sight behind her.
She walks toward a light and finds a lamp-post in the wood.
At the lamp-post, Lucy meets a strange person with goat legs, horns, parcels, and an umbrella.
The strange person is a Faun, but ch-01 does not name him.
Tier 3 reader knowledge does not include Tumnus, a cave, an errand, or the Witch yet.
The Faun is uncommitted at this point.
T3 does not promise that the Faun is safe or already Lucy's friend.
Ch-II's source ambiguity can still be fully honored at T3.
That includes the Faun being under the Witch's orders and later repenting.
The chapter ends on a real cliffhanger when the Faun cries out in surprise.

## ch-02 — What Lucy Found There
- work: narnia
- tier: 3
- chapter: ch-02
- status: promoted on muse-accept after tier-coordinator RECONCILED

A Tier 3 reader now also knows the following.
The Faun is named Mr. Tumnus; the land is Narnia (from the lamp-post to Cair Paravel).
Mr. Tumnus takes Lucy to his cosy cave for tea and tells her tales of forest life.
He plays a flute that nearly bewitches her; hours pass before she rouses to go home.
Mr. Tumnus breaks down and confesses he is in the pay of the White Witch.
The White Witch holds all Narnia under her thumb and makes it always winter and never Christmas.
Tumnus confesses he is a kidnapper: ordered to catch any human and hand them to the Witch.
Lucy is the first human he has met, and he had meant to lull her asleep and betray her.
He owns this choice openly; his wrongdoing is not externalized or excused (agency preserved at T3).
He repents because knowing Lucy changed him: "I hadn't known what Humans were like before I met you."
The Witch's cruelty is named but bounded: she might turn him to stone until the four thrones at Cair Paravel are filled.
The graphic mutilation detail (tail/horns/beard/hoofs) is cut at T3 ("no dwelling").
This plants the Cair-Paravel four-thrones prophecy seed for the first time at T3.
Tumnus leads Lucy quietly back through the spy-filled wood to the lamp-post; she sees the wardrobe door and gets home safely.
Lucy forgives him and lets him keep her handkerchief.
The ch-01 uncommitted-Faun ambiguity is now resolved: the errand and the repentance are both named.
The chapter ends on a genuine delayed-resolution thread: Tumnus is now at real risk from the Witch for disobeying.
T3 reader knowledge still does not include Aslan, the Deep Magic, Edmund's betrayal, or the Stone Table.
The work-level allegory is not named here (ch-02 is not a sacrifice chapter); the redemption meaning is event-carried.
