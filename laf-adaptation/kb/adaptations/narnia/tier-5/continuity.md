After Chapter I, a Tier-5 reader knows exactly what a source reader knows from Lewis's Chapter I as written.

Four children — Peter, Susan, Edmund, and Lucy — have been sent from London during the wartime air-raids to the old Professor's large country house. They meet the Professor and his housekeeper, Mrs. Macready, and anticipate exploring the large, strange house. On a rainy day, the children explore indoors. They pass through rooms containing pictures, a suit of armour, a green room with a harp, and a balcony, then enter an empty room containing only a big wardrobe.

Peter, Susan, and Edmund leave the wardrobe room, but Lucy stays behind. She opens the wardrobe, enters among the fur coats, and walks deeper, expecting to find the wooden back. Instead she finds cold snow underfoot and branches around her, emerging into a snowy night-time wood. She keeps the wardrobe doorway in sight and walks toward a distant light. The light is a lamp-post standing in the wood.

At the lamp-post, Lucy hears footsteps and meets a strange person identified by the narration as a Faun: goat-legged, horned, carrying parcels and an umbrella. The Faun is startled by Lucy, drops his parcels, and the chapter ends on his exclamation, "Goodness gracious me!"

Knowledge boundary preserved for ch-01: the Faun is met but not personally named in Chapter I; the source does not name him until Chapter II. Tier 5 makes no friend-promise, does not sanitize the Faun, and preserves the source's later moral complexity and ambiguity with no agency externalization.

After Chapter II, a Tier-5 reader knows exactly what a source reader knows from Lewis's Chapter II as written.

The Faun is named Mr. Tumnus. He confirms Lucy is a human ("Daughter of Eve"), tells her she is in Narnia, and invites her to tea at his cave. Over a splendid tea he tells her enchanting tales of forest life and plays a flute so bewitching that Lucy loses hours. When she rouses to leave, Tumnus breaks down and confesses: he is in the pay of the White Witch — who holds Narnia in endless winter, always winter and never Christmas — and was ordered to catch any human and hand them over. Lucy is the first, and he had meant to lull her asleep and betray her ("I'm doing it now, this very moment"). He describes the punishments the Witch inflicts on disobedient Fauns (tail cut off, horns sawn off, beard plucked, hoofs transformed, or being turned to stone until the four thrones at Cair Paravel are filled). But having come to know Lucy, he repents — "I hadn't known what Humans were like before I met you" — and leads her quietly back through the spy-ridden wood to the lamp-post. Lucy forgives him, they part, and she runs back through the wardrobe into the same empty room, calling out that she is back and all right.

Knowledge boundary preserved for ch-02: Tier 5 preserves the full confession, the full punishment list, Lucy's fright, and the repentance with no sanitization and no agency externalization. The White Witch, Aslan, and the Deep Magic beyond this chapter's content are not introduced here — only what Lewis's Chapter II asserts.
