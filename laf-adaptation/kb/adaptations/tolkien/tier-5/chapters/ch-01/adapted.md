Dawn never came to the plain that morning; the dark lord's shadow lay over the world like a
drawn curtain, and under it his host advanced upon the Grey City in a tide of iron. Sauron did
not ride among them, but every soldier felt the weight of his will pressing down, cold and
patient, from the black tower far to the east.

Upon the walls the Steward Denethor watched the enemy come, and something in him had already
broken. He had looked too long into the dark, and the dark had looked back. "All is ended," he
whispered, and would not answer when they called him, but turned his face to the wall.

Then the horns of the mountain-riders sounded, and Théoden the old king led his men down against
the host. The battle was terrible. Spears splintered and the ground ran wet, and one of the
Nazgûl — dark riders whose cry unmanned brave men — struck the king from his horse. Théoden fell, and did not rise again.

But Éowyn stood over him where he lay. She would not run. With her sword she met the rider and
struck it down, and the shadow of it screamed and was gone. Many good soldiers had fallen and
would not rise, and the field was heavy with grief; yet the city held, and the tide of iron
broke upon it and drew back toward the east. The sun, when at last it climbed above the smoke,
found the Grey City still standing.
