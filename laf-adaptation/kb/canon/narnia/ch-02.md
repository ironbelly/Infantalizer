## Source facts

- The Faun is named **Mr. Tumnus**. He confirms Lucy is a Human ("Daughter of Eve") — the first Human he has ever seen.
- Mr. Tumnus tells Lucy she is in the land of **Narnia** (all that lies between the lamp-post and the great castle of Cair Paravel on the eastern sea).
- Lucy says she came in through the wardrobe in the spare room; Tumnus mishears this as the far land of "Spare Oom" and the city of "War Drobe."
- Because it is winter and cold, Tumnus invites Lucy to tea at his cave; she accepts, saying she cannot stay long, and they walk arm-in-arm under his umbrella.
- Tumnus's cave is a warm, firelit home of reddish stone — carpet, two chairs, a table and dresser, a mantelpiece bearing a portrait of his grey-bearded father, and a shelf of books (including *Is Man a Myth?*).
- Tumnus lays a wonderful tea and tells Lucy tales of forest life: midnight dances with Nymphs and Dryads, hunts after the milk-white Stag (who grants wishes), feasting with the Red Dwarfs, and summer visits from Silenus and Bacchus when the streams ran with wine.
- Tumnus plays a strange straw flute whose tune makes Lucy want to cry, laugh, dance, and sleep at once; hours pass before she rouses to leave.
- Tumnus breaks down weeping and confesses he is a bad Faun: he has taken service in the pay of **the White Witch**, who holds all Narnia under her thumb and makes it "always winter and never Christmas."
- Tumnus confesses he is a kidnapper for the Witch: he was ordered to catch any Son of Adam or Daughter of Eve and hand them over. Lucy is the first he has met, and he had meant to lull her to sleep at tea and then betray her.
- Tumnus describes the punishments the Witch inflicts on a disobedient Faun: his tail cut off, his horns sawn off, his beard plucked out, his hoofs transformed, or being turned to stone — a statue until the four thrones at Cair Paravel are filled.
- Having come to know Lucy, Tumnus repents ("I hadn't known what Humans were like before I met you") and resolves he cannot give her up. He leads her quietly back through the spy-filled wood (even some trees are the Witch's), keeping to the darkest places, to the lamp-post.
- At the lamp-post Lucy can see the distant daylight of the wardrobe door; she forgives Tumnus, shakes his hand, hopes he won't get into trouble, and lets him keep her handkerchief.
- Lucy runs to the light, feels coats and wooden boards replace branches and snow, tumbles out of the wardrobe into the same empty room, and calls to the others that she is back and all right.
