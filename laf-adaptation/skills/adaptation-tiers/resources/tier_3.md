# Tier 3 — design commentary (ages 9-11, concrete operational)

*Rationale only — the authoritative T3 constraint data lives in `kb/tiers/tier_3.yaml`. Carried from
`docs/design_decisions/001-five-tier-system.md` and `003-agency-externalization.md`.*

## Why Tier 3 is the TRANSITION — "complexity unlocked"

Piaget places ages 9-11 in full **concrete operational** reasoning; Kohlberg reaches **stage 3**
(interpersonal conformity — good intentions, being seen as good). 001 calls Tier 3 the **natural
inflection point** of the whole system: it is where **full perspective-taking develops** (003), so the
child can, for the first time, understand **good people doing bad things** and hold genuine moral
tension. Complexity that was withheld at T1-2 is unlocked here.

## Agency Externalization becomes OPTIONAL — and why it must

This is the pivot in the Agency-Externalization ladder (MANDATORY T1-2 → **OPTIONAL T3** → FORBIDDEN
T4-5). Per 003, once full perspective-taking is online, **externalization becomes patronizing**: a
9-11-year-old who can already grasp motivation does not need "badness" relabeled as a temporary state,
and doing so unconditionally would talk down to them. So at T3 externalization is available as a tool but
no longer forced — the writer applies it selectively, and the safety rubric treats T3 as advisory
(report-only) rather than blocking.

## Why it is a scaffolded transition, not a hard switch

T3 sits between the protect-from-complexity tiers and the near-source tiers, so it **scaffolds**
complexity rather than dropping the child into it: the `tier_transition_summary` in `kb/tiers/tier_3.yaml`
records how thresholds relax from T2 toward T5. This mirrors 001's point that adjacent tiers have distinct
capabilities and T3 is the one that must bridge them.
