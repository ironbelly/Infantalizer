# Exemplar — Betrayal-and-Redemption (method illustration; NOT source truth)

## Challenge shape

The general pattern: a trusted figure betrays the protagonist (or the cause), the betrayal does real harm,
and the betrayer is later redeemed — through remorse, restitution, or sacrifice. The surface elements are
the act of betrayal, the harm it causes, and the arc of repair; the meaning is that betrayal wounds but does
not have the last word, and that a wrongdoer can be restored without the wrong being minimized. The
adaptation challenge is how to carry both the weight of the betrayal and the possibility of redemption at
each tier without either softening the wrong into nothing or hardening it past the possibility of repair.

## Method by tier

- **Tier 1:** Find the emotional core (someone trusted did the wrong thing, it hurt, and they chose to make
  it right). Decompose the surface — drop the most harming mechanics of the betrayal; keep a clear wrong
  done and a clear choice to repair it. Convert/name/preserve by tier: reframe the betrayal as a mistake or
  broken promise that is honestly owned and mended. Verify the meaning survived — both that a real wrong was
  done and that real repair happened; neither the betrayal nor the redemption is erased.
- **Tier 3:** Restore the betrayal as a genuine, costly breach of trust with named consequences, and the
  redemption as a hard-won, partial repair that doesn't undo all the harm. Verify meaning: the wound and the
  repair both remain in view.
- **Tier 5:** Preserve — betrayal, harm, and the full arc of redemption intact, surface and meaning both
  carried unchanged.

## Meaning that must survive

The betrayal-is-real-yet-repairable meaning — a true wrong is done and true repair becomes possible, and
neither half is collapsed into the other. Every tier changes the **surface** (how harming the betrayal is,
how much remorse is shown, how complete the repair is); no tier changes that **meaning**. A rendering that
treats the betrayal as no big deal (no wrong) or the betrayer as beyond repair (no redemption) is
`Meaning-DIFF`, regardless of how gentle or faithful its surface otherwise reads.
