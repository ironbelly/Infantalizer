# Exemplar — Petrification-Body-Horror (method illustration; NOT source truth)

## Challenge shape

The general pattern: a character is turned to stone (or otherwise frozen in their body) — a transformation
that is frightening, involuntary, and bodily. The surface elements are the transformation itself, the
visceral horror of being trapped in a changed body, and (often) the threat or hope of restoration; the
meaning is the loss and possible recovery of agency and selfhood, and the fear of being made into a thing.
The adaptation challenge is how to carry the threat and the dread at each tier — reframing the surface for
young readers without losing either the danger (this was a real harm) or the hope (restoration is possible).

## Method by tier

- **Tier 1:** Find the emotional core (someone is stuck and can't move or speak, and friends want to get
  them back). Decompose the surface — drop the body-horror imagery (the flesh-becoming-stone detail); keep a
  clear "frozen / playing-statues" state and a clear path to being un-frozen. Convert/name/preserve by tier:
  reframe petrification as a game of statues or a sleep-like stillness that the heroes can reverse. Verify
  the meaning survived — the danger of being trapped and the hope of rescue both remain; only the horrific
  surface changed.
- **Tier 3:** Restore the petrification as real and frightening (genuinely turned to stone, genuinely a
  harm) with bounded, non-gruesome framing; the dread is named but not lingered on. Verify meaning: the
  threat and the hope both remain in view.
- **Tier 5:** Preserve — the full petrification/body-horror surface and its meaning intact, carried
  unchanged.

## Meaning that must survive

The trapped-yet-restorable meaning — a real loss of agency and selfhood that is genuinely frightening and
genuinely reversible. Every tier changes the **surface** (how visceral the transformation is, how much dread
is shown, how the restoration is framed); no tier changes that **meaning**. A rendering that treats being
turned to stone as a harmless prank (no danger) or as permanent and un-rescuable (no hope) is `Meaning-DIFF`,
regardless of how gentle or faithful its surface otherwise reads.
