# Exemplar — Sacrifice-and-Return (method illustration; NOT source truth)

## Challenge shape

The general pattern: a character gives their life for another (or for a cause), dies, and then returns —
the death is real but not final. The surface elements are a binding/judgment, a death, and a restoration; the
meaning is redemptive substitution freely chosen. The adaptation challenge is how much of the death to show
or imply at each tier without losing either the cost (it was a real death) or the hope (it was overcome).

## Method by tier

- **Tier 1:** Find the emotional core (someone choosing to give up everything so another can live, and that
  being made right). Decompose the surface into its elements — drop the binding/knife/death imagery; convert
  the death to a long rest or sleep taken on another's behalf. Convert/name/preserve by tier: "traded a long
  rest to fix a mistake; woke warm." Verify the meaning survived — the choice and the restoration still carry
  the redemptive-substitution meaning; only the lethal surface changed.
- **Tier 3:** Restore the death as a real death with gentler framing — the character genuinely dies and
  returns by a deeper magic / grace; stakes are named but not dwelt on. Verify meaning: the cost and the hope
  both remain.
- **Tier 5:** Preserve — the sacrificial death and resurrection intact, surface and meaning both carried
  unchanged.

## Meaning that must survive

The redemptive-substitution meaning — a freely chosen self-giving that is honored and overcome. Every tier
changes the **surface** (lethal imagery, framing, how much is shown); no tier changes that **meaning**. A
rendering that turns the death into an accident (no choice) or the return into a trick (no cost) is
`Meaning-DIFF`, regardless of how gentle or faithful its surface otherwise reads.
