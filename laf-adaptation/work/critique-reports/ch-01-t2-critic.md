## Significant

1. **line 7** — "Somewhere outside, an owl called… 'Let us explore tomorrow!' said Peter. There
   might be woods and hills and eagles." The sequence jumps grumpy-talk → owl → plan → "eagles"
   abruptly. **Cost:** choppy read-aloud flow; the outdoor promise doesn't build as one warm
   anticipation beat. **Fix:** one gently mounting bedtime moment — grumbles fading, owl calling,
   Peter imagining tomorrow.
2. **line 11** — "They found… They found… They found…" repetitive list structure. **Cost:** the
   magical old-house curiosity flattens to inventory, losing the "house you never come to the end
   of" reward. **Fix:** vary sentence shapes; let one or two rooms carry sensory/playful detail,
   keep it brisk.
3. **lines 15–21** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) — "It was cold and white and powdery. Snow! Something rough
   brushed her face…" The wardrobe→wood transition is faithful but checklist-like at the chapter's
   central wonder beat. **Cost:** the reader understands the magic before feeling Lucy discover it;
   the portal loses awe. **Fix:** slow a few beats through Lucy's body/uncertainty — softness
   underfoot, cold on fingers, prickling branches, the impossible light ahead.
4. **line 21** — "'I can always get home if I need to,' she thought. So she walked on…" resolves
   the unease too neatly. **Cost:** mild tension drains just when the wood should feel
   safe-but-strange. **Fix:** keep the open door as reassurance, but leave a trace of
   wonder/hesitation before she chooses the light.
5. **lines 25–27** — the Faun description is staged like a head/body/legs/accessories diagram.
   **Cost:** the reveal loses read-aloud sparkle and comic surprise. **Fix:** reorder through
   Lucy's noticing — umbrella/parcels first, then the startling hoofs and horns, one rhythmically
   satisfying comic image.

## Minor

1. **line 3** — "sent away from the city because of the war" is functional; add one small
   child-facing detail of leaving/arriving without darkening the tone.
2. **line 5** — "shaggy white hair all over his face" tilts toward T1/cartoonish; mature to
   shaggy hair + beard/eyebrows, odd-and-impressive not silly.
3. **line 9** — "the rain came down and down" echoes T1 repetition; use a richer-but-simple T2
   phrase (rain tapping/streaming at the windows).
4. **line 15** — the "silly to shut yourself inside a wardrobe" aside is charming but slightly
   didactic here; tuck the safety idea into Lucy's action so the coats keep drawing her inward.
5. **line 31** — "'Goodness gracious me!' said the Faun" — "said" underplays the comic shock;
   use a livelier (not frightening) tag aligned with the source's "exclaimed".

## Verdict
Not promotion-ready as v1 — close and fundamentally faithful, but needs ONE line/voice revision
pass focused on the wonder beat (S3), sentence-flow variety (S1/S2/S5), and reducing T1-like
repetition (Minors 2/3). No structural surgery.
