**Reader:** 6–8yo, early-concrete-operational, read-aloud or reading-with-help. Wider emotion

still needs warmth/safety. **Draft:** `work/drafts/ch-01-t2-v2.md`.

## Strongest lean-in / wonder moments
1. **The wardrobe crossing (peak).** Fingers finding "only more soft coats," then the floor
   changes — "Something soft crunched under her shoes... cold, and it prickled her fingers.
   Snow?" A 6–8yo has real referents for all of it and gets the dawning realization a beat
   before Lucy. The single "Snow?" question lets the child solve it — the strongest lean-in.
2. **The lamp-post in the wood.** "How strange, to find a lamp-post among the trees!" A
   concrete-operational child registers it as a genuine puzzle (lamp-posts belong on streets).
3. **The Faun reveal, top-down.** Umbrella/parcels/"as if he had been shopping" first, then the
   surprise turn to goat-legs and "two neat little hoofs!" Paced for a small twist; horns
   "peeped out" keeps it charming, not monstrous.

## Safety / unsafe moments (all resolve or are permitted mild tension)
- **Dark wood at night** — mild tension (T2-permitted), caught immediately by the safety rope:
  the open wardrobe door "still glowed... 'I can always go back.'" Resolves; strange not trapping.
- **"Someone was coming" + the Faun's jump** — a small frisson flipped by the Faun being the
  startled one; the child feels proud/protective, not scared.
- **Mild-hook ending** — "'Goodness gracious me!' exclaimed the Faun" leaves the child EAGER
  (who is he?), not anxious. Correct T2 `can_end_on_mild_tension`.
- **Possible hard words** (solo reader): "exclaimed," "mothballs," "harp," "suit of armour,"
  "woollen." None are safety issues; safest as read-aloud / read-with-help (matches persona).

## Emotional arc
Settled/safe → cozy indoor exploring → curiosity peak (Lucy stays behind) → wonder + mild tension
(crossing) → reassurance (door glows) → puzzle-delight (lamp-post) → small frisson → warm comic
surprise (the Faun). Rises into gentle tension, lands on charm.

## Knowledge boundary (FLAG TO CHRONICLER)
The Faun is **met but NOT named**, and — unlike the T1 draft — T2 does **NOT** pre-commit him as
"kind/friend." He is "very strange," startled, un-vouched-for. This is a CLEANER boundary than T1:
the reader leaves curious-but-uncommitted about whether the Faun is safe, preserving room for the
source's later ambiguity. **T2 has NOT promised the Faun is safe** (contrast the T1 promise).

## Verdict: **Yes** — lands for a 6–8yo. More atmosphere and a genuine mild hook than T1; every
alert resolves or is comically defused; ends eager not anxious; Faun left unnamed and uncommitted.
