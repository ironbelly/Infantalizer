## Significant

1. **line 3** — "Their new home was the house of an old Professor, deep in the country." Opening
   compression loses Lewis's "sent away" cadence and "heart of the country" texture. **Cost:**
   reads informational, not narrated; weakens the Lewis-like invitation. **Fix:** restore a more
   cumulative opening rhythm and the source's formal phrasing ("sent away from London during the
   war…" / "in the heart of the country…").
2. **lines 23–25** — cutting Peter's "ten minutes' walk… any amount of stairs and passages" boast
   removes the comic exaggeration that feeds Lucy's creepiness. **Cost:** the house feels less
   enormous; Lucy's unease and the exploration lose atmospheric buildup. **Fix:** reintroduce a
   shortened version of the boast, then let Lucy's "long passages and rows of doors" thought land.
3. **lines 45–47** (HIGHEST PRIORITY) — the house-exploration is chopped into report-like
   waypoints, losing Lewis's rolling "and then… and then…" discovery momentum. **Cost:** a list of
   rooms instead of the sensation of wandering deeper into a marvelous, inexhaustible house.
   **Fix:** allow one longer cumulative sentence here, keeping vocabulary accessible.
4. **lines 53–57** — Lucy's tactile temptation is slightly flattened; the draft drops
   "immediately," "soft folds," "to make room for her." **Cost:** the crossing becomes a sequence
   of actions rather than a gradual sensuous yielding to curiosity. **Fix:** restore the source's
   tactile continuity (getting among the coats, pushing soft folds aside, making room).
5. **lines 61–65** — "It was a long way off. Something cold and soft was falling on her." The
   magical transition is over-separated; splitting reduces the uncanny glide. **Cost:** the wonder
   lands clearly but less enchantingly — discrete reveals, not one dawning impossibility. **Fix:**
   combine select beats into a longer source-like reveal (light far off → cold softness falling →
   the wood at night).

## Minor

2. **lines 37–41** — the breakfast/upstairs-room setting and Susan's "Ten to one" idiom are

3. **line 49** — "dead fly" is less Lewis-textured than "dead blue-bottle"; at T3 (grade 3–5)

4. **lines 69–73** — the Faun reveal loses a few narratorly turns ("wondering what to do next,"

## Verdict
Needs a revision pass before promotion. For T3 "near-Lewis fidelity," the prose is still too
often controlled simplification rather than faithful adapted texture — several Lewis narrator
